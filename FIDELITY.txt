ERDOS 684: INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL AND SOURCE AUDIT
18 October 2026

Scope
-----
This audit concerns Finite.lean, BadPrimes.lean, Averaging.lean,
Analytic.lean, Finish.lean and Lower.lean in research/erdos684.
It is a mathematical and statement audit, not a compilation certificate.
Compiler and axiom reports for the final snapshot must be recorded separately.

Two key verdicts
----------------
1. The finite weighted averaging argument is mathematically correct. The
   proposed elementary estimates yield the uniform eventual upper bound with
   constant 514, without assuming the prime number theorem.
2. The prime-power obstruction and elementary Chebyshev estimates yield the
   infinitely-often lower obstruction with coefficient 1/8. This is an
   infinite-sequence assertion, not an eventual lower bound for all inputs.

Exact source-to-Lean contract
-----------------------------
smallPrimePart n k is the product over primes p <= k of
  p ^ ((n.choose k).factorization p).
It includes full prime-power multiplicity. It is not a squarefree kernel.
The non-strict cutoff matches the modern problem page and APSSV26 Section 2.
The original Erdos article's printed page 74 uses a strict p < k cutoff;
the modern convention is an explicit difference, documented in SOURCES.md.

upper_bound asserts:
  for all sufficiently large natural n, there exists a natural k <= n with
  (k : Real) <= 514 * (log n)^2 and n^2 < smallPrimePart n k.
The eventual threshold is absolute, independent of n and the selected k.
The proof actually chooses k >= 1. Its public existential need not state
that separately: for positive n, k = 0 cannot satisfy the strict threshold.

frequently_smallPrimePart_eq_one asserts:
  for arbitrarily large natural n, n > 1 and for every natural k,
  (k : Real) <= log n / 8 implies smallPrimePart n k = 1.
On the constructed inputs the relevant k also satisfy k <= n. Since n > 1,
the value 1 is strictly below n^2, so no such k is an upper-bound witness.

These direct assertions avoid defining the minimum f(n) on small inputs
where its defining set can be empty. If f is defined for a large input,
the first theorem bounds it from above and the second bounds it from below
on the indicated infinite family. They establish the known qualitative
orders O(log^2 n) above and Omega(log n) infinitely often below. They do not
prove f(n) = Theta(log n), an asymptotic formula, or unbounded f(n)/log n.

APSSV26 Theorem 2.1 has stronger constants: upper coefficient
24/(pi^2-6) + o(1), and infinite-sequence lower coefficient 1/2 + o(1).
Those sharper constants are NOT claimed here. The current independent
implementation adapts the same residue strategy with coarse Chebyshev
constants. Its weaker explicit constants are deliberate, not a missing
hypothesis hidden in the public statements. No new mathematical discovery
is claimed. Cite Alexeev, Putterman, Sawhney, Sellke and Valiant and the
paper's attribution of its proofs to an internal OpenAI model.

Upper argument audit
--------------------
Let L = log n, X = ceil(256 L^2), and A = ceil(32 L).
The analytic parameter lemma arranges, uniformly for all sufficiently large n:
  L >= 1, n > 0, 2X <= n, A <= n,
  32L <= A <= 33L, 256L^2 <= X <= 257L^2,
  log(n+A) <= 2L, theta(X) >= X/2, and 2X <= 514L^2.
All ceilings are controlled by their unit rounding error. The inequalities
L/n -> 0 and L^2/n -> 0 make the natural-number window admissible.
Chebyshev.theta_ge' gives theta(x) >= x/2 eventually because its main
coefficient is log 2 > 1/2 and its logarithmic/square-root errors are o(x).

Partition primes p <= X into bad primes with p - n%p <= A and good primes
with A < p - n%p. For every bad prime the positive gap
j = p - n%p belongs to [1,A], and p divides n+j. Since the bad primes are
distinct, their product divides the positive product of n+1,...,n+A.
Taking logarithms gives bad weight <= A log(n+A), including A = 0.
Thus good weight >= theta(X) - A log(n+A).

For a fixed good prime p put r = n%p. For each i in [1,A] take
  k = p + r + i.
The strict good-prime condition ensures r+i < p, hence
  k%p = r+i > r, and p <= k < 2p <= 2X <= n.
The first-level carry therefore gives binomial valuation at least one.
The map i -> k is injective, so this prime contributes at least A log p
over the full window [1,2X]. Distinct primes can contribute at the same k;
their weights add and do not require disjoint k-windows. primeWeight has
an explicit p <= k guard, preventing primes above the small-factor cutoff
from being counted. Swapping the finite sums and discarding nonnegative
unused weights gives
  A(theta(X) - A log(n+A)) <= sum_{1 <= k <= 2X} log(smallPrimePart n k).

The explicit numerical margin is:
  theta(X) >= 128L^2,
  A log(n+A) <= 66L^2,
  A(theta(X) - A log(n+A)) >= 32L * 62L^2 = 1984L^3,
whereas 4XL <= 1028L^3.
Since L >= 1, the inequality is strict in the required direction.
If every window term had smallPrimePart <= n^2, positivity and logarithm
monotonicity would give each log term <= 2L. There are 2X terms, so their
sum would be <= 4XL, contradicting the averaging bound. Some admissible
k therefore has smallPrimePart > n^2 and k <= 514L^2.

Lower argument audit
--------------------
For K > 2 let M = lcmUpto(K) * primorial(K), and n = M-1.
For each prime p <= K, lcmUpto(K) contains p^(Nat.log p K), while the
primorial contributes one further p. Hence p^(Nat.log p K + 1) divides M
and is strictly larger than K.
For every k <= K, comparison of residues modulo every power of such a p
rules out all carries in the binomial coefficient (M-1).choose k.
All its relevant prime valuations vanish, giving smallPrimePart n k = 1.
This uses the corrected non-strict comparison p^e-1 >= K, including its
possible equality case. No assertion about primes above K is needed.

The exact identities log M = psi(K) + theta(K), and Mathlib's elementary
bounds psi(K) <= (log 4 + 4)K and theta(K) <= (log 4)K, give
  log M <= (2 log 4 + 4)K <= 8K.
Consequently log n / 8 <= K, and the finite obstruction covers the whole
claimed logarithmic window. The elementary primorial inequality gives
K < M, so n >= K. Taking K larger than an arbitrary natural cutoff proves
the required unboundedness of the constructed n, not just repeated use of
one example. The condition n > 1 makes its obstruction nondegenerate.
